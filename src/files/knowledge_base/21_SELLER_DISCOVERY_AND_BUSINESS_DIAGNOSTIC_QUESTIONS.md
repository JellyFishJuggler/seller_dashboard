# Seller Discovery and Business Diagnostic Questions

This document teaches how a Key Account Manager (KAM) runs a seller conversation that discovers the real problem: finding the seller's main concern, asking the right diagnostic question at the right time, adapting to answers, and handling missing information. Examples use the assignment sample seller (sales ₹2,00,000 to ₹1,35,000, orders 400 to 270, cancellation 8% to 14%, ASP ₹500, out-of-stock 5 to 18) as illustrative data only. Conversations include natural English and Hinglish.

## 1. Goals of discovery

1. Understand what the seller worries about and cares about.
2. Test hypotheses using the data already available, before asking the seller for it.
3. Find facts only the seller knows: recent changes, supplier issues, packing practice, promotions.
4. Build a shared view of the problem before recommending actions.

## 2. Start from data, not from a blank page

Never ask the seller for something the metrics already show. Open with the observation, then ask. Good: "Your orders dropped from 400 to 270 and stockouts rose from 5 to 18. Which of your top products have run out?" Poor: "What is your sales?"

## 3. One relevant question at a time

Voice conversations suffer when multiple questions are stacked. Ask a single question, listen, then follow up. Choose the question that best separates the leading hypotheses.

## 4. Choosing the next question: a simple order

1. Confirm the facts (periods, definitions) only if uncertain.
2. Ask which products or area the seller thinks is affected.
3. Test availability: stock status of top products.
4. Test cancellations: reasons.
5. Test demand and visibility: what changed, impressions if available.
6. Test pricing and promotions.
7. Test operations: processing speed, packing.
8. Ask about priorities and constraints before proposing actions.

Re-order based on what the seller says and what data is available.

## 5. Avoid redundant questions

Track what has been asked and answered; do not ask again. Use tools data first. If the seller already said "stock was low on my bestseller", move to which products and since when.

## 6. Following up on answers

- Vague answer ("sales are slow"): quantify gently ("Your orders are down about a third; does that match what you see?").
- Confident cause ("it's the courier"): test politely ("Have you seen cancellation reasons that say pickup failed?") instead of agreeing or disputing.
- Unknown ("pata nahi"): offer a way to find out ("Can you check the cancelled orders list and tell me the top reason?").

## 7. Understanding seller priorities

Ask what matters most: recovering sales fast, protecting profit, cleaning operations, or growing the range. Priorities decide which of 2-3 actions to propose first (KB-23). Ask about constraints: cash, staff time, supplier lead time.

## 8. Handling incomplete information

State what you can and cannot tell from the data. Offer interim hypotheses with confidence levels (KB-25). Ask for the one piece of missing data that matters most. Do not fill gaps with guesses.

## 9. Adapting questions by category

- **Apparel**: sizes out of stock, size-wise sales, size-related returns, size chart.
- **Home decor**: damage returns, packaging, dimensions, colour accuracy.
- **Mobile accessories**: compatibility returns, model variants, authenticity, IMEI/serial capture needs.
- **General**: top products, stock, cancellation reasons, recent changes.

## 10. Question bank by problem area

**Sales falling**
- Which products usually bring most of your orders?
- Did those products sell less, or did all products slow down?
- Was anything different this month (price, promotion, packing, supplier)?

**Stockouts**
- Which items are out of stock now, and since when?
- How long does restocking take?
- Is the panel quantity updated by hand?

**Cancellations**
- What are the main reasons you see for cancelled orders?
- Do cancelled orders belong to products that were short on stock?
- How soon after an order do you pack it?

**Visibility/conversion**
- Do you see views or clicks per product?
- Did you change any title or images?

**Returns**
- What are your top return reasons?
- Which product returns most?

## 11. Example conversation 1 (English): opening the diagnosis

KAM: "I looked at your last two months. Orders went from 400 to 270, and your price stayed at ₹500 on average, so the drop is in the number of orders, not the price. Also, out-of-stock products went from 5 to 18. Which of your best-selling products are out of stock right now?"
Seller: "I think three of my top items are finished."
KAM: "Which ones, and since roughly when?"
Seller: "The steel bottle and the two lamps, around two weeks."
KAM: "Understood. Before we decide what to do, how many of those usually sell per day?" (Business reasoning: confirms availability hypothesis and gathers demand to rank stockouts, KB-09.)

## 12. Example conversation 2 (Hinglish): cancellations

KAM: "Aapki cancellation rate 8% se 14% ho gayi hai. Panel mein cancel hone ke reasons dikh rahe hain kya?"
Seller: "Haan, kuch 'seller cancelled' dikh raha hai."
KAM: "Samajh gaya. Kya woh orders un products ke hain jinka stock kam tha?"
Seller: "Shayad haan, do-teen products ka stock panel mein zyada dikha raha tha."
KAM: "Theek hai, to ek possible reason stock mismatch hai. Panel ka stock aap kitni baar update karte hain?"
(Business reasoning: classify cancellations before proposing a fix, KB-14; does not assume fault.)

## 13. Example conversation 3 (Hinglish): seller blames price

Seller: "Sales kam hui kyunki competitor sasta hai."
KAM: "Ho sakta hai. Lekin aapka average selling price ₹500 hi raha, aur orders 400 se 270 hue. Kya aapne dekha ki kaunse products ke orders sabse zyada gire?"
Seller: "Mujhe exact nahi pata."
KAM: "Koi baat nahi. Agar aap batayein ki top 3 products ke orders kitne the pehle aur ab, to hum dekh sakte hain ki price ki baat sahi hai ya stock ki."
(Business reasoning: respects the seller's view, tests it with data.)

## 14. Example conversation 4 (apparel)

KAM: "Aapke kurti ke returns badh rahe hain. Return reasons mein 'size' kitni baar aa raha hai?"
Seller: "Zyada tar 'too small'."
KAM: "Kaun sa size sabse zyada return hota hai?"
Seller: "L aur XL."
KAM: "To size chart aur actual garment measurement match karte hain, yeh check karna hoga. Kya aapne chart mein body size diya hai ya garment size?"

## 15. Closing a discovery round

Summarise in plain language: what the data shows, what you think is likely (with the degree of certainty), what is still unknown, and what you suggest checking. Then move toward 2-3 actions and ask for agreement (KB-23).

## 16. What not to do

- Do not ask three questions at once.
- Do not ask what the tools already show.
- Do not lecture or list every possible cause.
- Do not end discovery with a generic "improve your listings".

## 17. What not to conclude

- Do not decide the root cause from the first answer.
- Do not assume the seller's explanation is wrong or right without data.
