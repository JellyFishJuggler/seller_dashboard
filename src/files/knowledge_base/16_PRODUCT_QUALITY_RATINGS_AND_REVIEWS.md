# Product Quality, Ratings and Reviews

Ratings and reviews are both a signal of product quality and a driver of buyer trust. This document explains how to analyse customer feedback, find recurring complaints, improve quality and manage reviews **ethically**. Fake, purchased, manipulated or incentivised reviews are never recommended: they harm buyers, violate marketplace rules in general and carry legal and account risks. Platform review policies must be confirmed from official sources (KB-24).

## 1. Why feedback matters

- Buyers read ratings and recent reviews before buying (conversion, KB-12).
- Reviews expose problems the seller cannot see, such as fit, finish, packaging, compatibility.
- Review themes predict returns.

## 2. Reading ratings correctly

- The average hides the distribution. A 4.0 average can be many 5-star and many 1-star reviews (polarising product) or consistent 4-star.
- Check review count: an average on 5 reviews is weak evidence.
- Look at the trend: ratings in the last 30 days versus all time. A sudden fall after a supplier or packaging change is informative.
- Separate product reviews from delivery or courier complaints.

## 3. Feedback analysis method

1. Collect reviews and return reasons for a product over a period.
2. Tag each by theme: quality/defect, size/fit, colour mismatch, material, packaging/damage, compatibility, delivery, wrong item, value for money, other.
3. Count by theme and by variant.
4. Rank themes by frequency and severity.
5. Find the root cause per theme (supplier batch, packing process, listing content, courier).
6. Fix and track whether the theme declines.

Example (illustrative): 40 reviews, 18 negative. Themes among negatives: packaging damage 7, colour different from photo 6, delivery delay 3, quality 2. Packaging and colour accuracy are the main fixable items.

## 4. Recurring complaints

A single complaint may be random; the same complaint repeated by several buyers is a pattern. Recurring complaints should become actions: update the listing (honest description), change packaging, switch supplier, add inspection, discontinue a product.

## 5. Quality control

- Inspect incoming stock (sample or full, depending on risk).
- Check batch consistency (colour, size, finish).
- Keep supplier records; raise defects with suppliers.
- Test critical functions (electronics, zips, seams, charging).
- Final check before packing.
- Isolate defective units.

## 6. Packaging quality

Packaging protects, presents and prevents returns. Match packaging to product fragility and shipping rough handling. Test with a drop or shake check for fragile products. Do not cut packaging cost without checking damage rates.

## 7. Expectation mismatch

When reviews say "not as shown" or "different from description", the listing is the problem. Fix photos, colour description, dimensions and materials. Expect conversion to adjust slightly while returns and ratings improve.

## 8. Responding to reviews and complaints ethically

Allowed and good:
- Reply to genuine feedback where the platform allows, politely and factually.
- Offer genuine solutions (replacement or refund through proper platform channels).
- Use reviews to improve the product.
- Encourage satisfied customers to share honest feedback in ways allowed by platform rules (for example, quality packaging and a neutral insert, only if platform rules permit; confirm first).

Not allowed:
- Writing or buying fake reviews.
- Offering gifts or discounts in exchange for positive reviews.
- Asking buyers to remove negative reviews in exchange for compensation.
- Using multiple accounts to rate your own products.
- Attacking competitors with fake negative reviews.

If a review seems abusive or fake, use the platform's reporting channel.

## 9. Product improvement cycles

1. Gather feedback and returns monthly.
2. Identify the top 1-3 issues.
3. Decide a fix (listing, supplier, packaging, process).
4. Implement.
5. Compare the next period: ratings of new reviews, return reasons, return rate.
6. Document and repeat.

## 10. Questions a KAM should ask

- What do your recent negative reviews say?
- Are the complaints about the product, packaging or delivery?
- Did ratings change after any supplier, batch or packaging change?
- How do you check items before dispatch?
- Which complaint appears most often?

## 11. What not to conclude

- Do not conclude a product is bad from a few reviews.
- Do not suggest manipulating reviews in any way.
- Do not blame the seller for courier-caused reviews without checking.
- Do not predict rating recovery timing.
