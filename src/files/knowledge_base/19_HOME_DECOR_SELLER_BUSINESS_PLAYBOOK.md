# Home Decor Seller Business Playbook

This playbook covers the business issues specific to selling home decor and home furnishing items online: dimensions, materials, fragile-product packaging, colour accuracy, photography, damage-related returns and seasonal demand. Guidance is general; platform packaging or listing requirements must be confirmed from official sources (KB-24). Scenarios are illustrative.

## 1. What makes home decor different

- Buyers judge size and look in their own space, so dimensions and colour matter greatly.
- Many items are fragile (ceramic, glass, resin) or bulky (frames, planters).
- Purchases are often gifts or festive, creating seasonal peaks.
- Product photography drives both clicks and expectations.

## 2. Dimensions and measurements

State length, width, height, diameter, weight and, where relevant, capacity, in cm (and inches). Say whether dimensions are of the product or the packed parcel. Add a scale reference photo (object beside a hand or a common item). Check measurements against the real item for each batch. Wrong size is a frequent cause of "not as expected" returns.

## 3. Material and finish

Specify material (ceramic, wood type, metal, resin, fabric) and finish (matte, glossy, hand-painted, distressed). Do not call something "pure" or "handmade" unless true. Mention natural variation for handcrafted goods so buyers expect minor differences. Include care instructions.

## 4. Colour accuracy and photography

- Use consistent natural lighting.
- Photograph the actual product, not renders only.
- Show the item in context for scale and also a plain background shot for true colour.
- Avoid heavy filters; note that screen colour can differ slightly.
Colour or look mismatch is a leading expectation-mismatch reason (KB-15, KB-16).

## 5. Product descriptions

Describe what is included (pack count, accessories), use cases, placement ideas, assembly needs, care. Be specific about what is not included (for example, "bulb not included").

## 6. Fragile-product packaging

Damage in transit is the main cost driver for fragile items.

Packaging practices:
- Use a rigid outer carton sized to the item; avoid extra empty space.
- Wrap each item individually with cushioning (bubble wrap, foam, paper).
- Fill void space so the item does not move.
- Protect corners and edges for frames and glass.
- Use double-boxing for very fragile items where cost allows.
- Mark fragile and "this side up" (couriers may not honour them, so do not rely on labels alone).
- Seal properly with strong tape.

Quick test: pack the item and shake or do a drop test from a modest height before adopting a packing method.

## 6b. Reducing damage-related returns: method

1. Count damage returns by product and by packaging type.
2. Identify the highest-damage products and compare their packaging.
3. Check whether damages cluster by courier, region or time.
4. Photograph returned items to see damage patterns (broken corners, crushed carton).
5. Improve packaging for the worst products first; test.
6. Compare damage rates before and after for a similar period and volume.
7. Calculate the cost trade-off: extra packaging cost per order versus avoided return cost.

Example (illustrative): packaging cost rises ₹20 per order; damage returns fall from 8% to 3% on 100 orders. Extra cost: 100 × 20 = ₹2,000. Avoided damages: 5 returns × (product cost ₹250 + shipping ₹100) = ₹1,750. The saving alone is slightly less than the cost in this example, but ratings, reviews and repeat orders also improve; the test shows what is worth keeping and where packaging can be cheaper. Use real figures.

## 7. Damage-related returns: other levers

- Choose products designed for shipping (less delicate designs).
- Raise packaging only for fragile SKUs.
- Insert care or "unbox carefully" card.
- Inspect before dispatch; photograph packed item as evidence.
- Escalate repeated courier damage with evidence.
- Whether the platform or the courier bears the cost in a damage case is platform-specific; confirm with seller support.

## 8. Customer expectations

Decor buyers expect the item to look as in the photos, arrive intact, be of the stated size and fit their space. Disappointment comes from small size, different shade, visible flaws, assembly surprises and damage. Honest content and strong packaging address most of these.

## 9. Seasonal demand

Peaks around festivals, weddings, housewarming seasons, and sale events. Plan stock and packaging supplies ahead; check lead times from suppliers; build seasonal assortments (lights, torans, idols, gifting items); plan post-season clearance. Without prior-year data, begin conservatively.

## 10. Home decor assortment planning

- Balance price tiers (entry gifting items, mid-range decor, premium).
- Offer sets and bundles (a set of three planters) with logistics in mind.
- Keep fragile and non-fragile mix to manage risk.
- Avoid too many near-duplicate designs.
- Track product-level damage and return rates to guide assortment decisions.

## 11. Realistic scenarios

**Scenario A: ceramic vase damage.** 9% of vases are returned as broken. Packaging is a thin carton with a single layer of paper. After switching to a snug box with foam and corner protection, the damage rate falls. The seller tracks the return rate and packaging cost.

**Scenario B: colour mismatch.** A teal wall-hanging looks blue in the photo. Returns "different colour" rise. Reshoot in daylight with a true-colour shot; update colour name.

**Scenario C: size surprise.** A "large" planter is 18 cm. Buyers expected larger. Add dimensions and a scale photo.

## 12. Questions a KAM should ask

- Which products are returned for damage, and how are they packed?
- Do damages cluster with a courier or region?
- Do your listings show exact dimensions and scale photos?
- Do photos show true colour?
- Which items are your seasonal bestsellers, and when does their season begin?

## 13. What not to conclude

- Do not say damage is the courier's fault without evidence of packaging adequacy.
- Do not state platform rules for damage reimbursement as facts.
- Do not recommend a specific packaging spend without damage and cost data.
