# Mobile Accessories Seller Business Playbook

This playbook covers selling phone cases, chargers, cables, earphones, screen guards, power banks and similar items online: compatibility, model-specific variants, connector specifications, authenticity, warranty, packaging and quality. It is general guidance. **It does not state any legal or platform-specific requirement.** Platform rules (for example documentation requirements, IMEI or serial number capture for certain products, brand authorisation) differ by platform and category and must be confirmed from official sources (KB-24). Scenarios are illustrative.

## 1. What makes this category different

- Compatibility is the main purchase risk: the wrong model or connector makes the item useless.
- Many variants per product (models, colours, lengths).
- Many look-alike products; buyers worry about fake or low-quality items.
- Low price points mean fixed shipping and return costs hit margins hard (KB-06).
- Technical specs matter (wattage, cable length, connector, battery capacity).

## 2. Device compatibility and model-specific variants

Cases and screen guards are model-specific (for example by exact phone model, and even by variant of the model). Chargers and cables depend on connector type and supported charging speeds.

Practices:
- List each model as a separate, clearly labelled variant.
- Use full model names exactly as buyers know them.
- Do not use vague terms such as "universal" unless it is accurate.
- Keep stock per model; demand differs heavily by model and fades as models age.
- Retire variants for very old models when demand is gone.

## 3. Connector specifications and technical content

State: connector types at both ends (USB Type-C, Lightning, micro-USB, others), cable length, supported power (watts) and data transfer if true, materials, compatible device list, certifications you can truthfully claim. Add a connector close-up image. Avoid claims such as "fast charging" without stating the conditions (the device and charger must also support it).

## 4. Technical product descriptions

Write what the product does and does not do. Include box contents, specifications, compatibility, and cautions. Use plain language along with spec terms buyers search for.

## 5. Compatibility-related returns: reduction method

1. Break returns by reason and model, product and listing. Tag "doesn't fit", "not compatible", "wrong connector", "doesn't charge my device", "wrong model sent".
2. Separate **listing causes** (ambiguous title, missing model in title, vague "compatible with" text) from **fulfilment causes** (wrong variant packed) and **product causes** (item genuinely doesn't fit as claimed).
3. Check which listings have the highest compatibility-return rates.
4. Fix listings: put exact models or connector types in the title; add a compatibility list; add comparison photos of the connector.
5. Fix fulfilment: label shelves by model, verify the variant at packing.
6. Check the product: test sample fit on the actual devices before listing.
7. Track return rate by reason after changes.

Example (illustrative): 50 returns of a case listing. 30 say "doesn't fit", of which most buyers own a variant of the model (for example Pro versus standard). The title says only the family name. Fix: list the exact models in separate variants.

## 6. Product authenticity

Buyers worry about counterfeit chargers and branded accessories. Only sell genuine branded products if you can prove sourcing; keep supplier invoices. Do not misuse brand names in titles for unbranded products ("compatible with" phrasing is different from claiming the brand). Brand authorisation or documents that a platform may require must be confirmed from official sources; do not assume. Counterfeits risk account action and legal consequences, and harm buyer safety (poor chargers and batteries can be dangerous).

## 7. Warranty information

State warranty only if real: duration, who honours it (brand, seller), how to claim, and what it excludes. A false or unclear warranty promise creates disputes. Keep proof of purchase and the supplier's warranty terms.

## 8. Serial and IMEI-related considerations

For some electronics, platforms may require recording identifiers such as IMEI or serial numbers at the time of dispatch. The official Snapdeal API documentation opened for this knowledge base mentions that IMEI is required for products such as mobile phones and serial numbers for some computer accessories such as hard drives and chargers (KB-24). Its currency is unverified, and the category list is not complete. Ask the seller what their category requires, and confirm with seller support. Where recording is required, build a packing step to capture identifiers and keep records for returns.

## 9. Packaging

Use adequate protective packaging: cables and small items need tidy retail packaging and sturdy outer packs; screen guards need rigid protection; power banks and batteries have shipping restrictions that courier partners may apply. Do not assume specifics; check with couriers or seller support. Keep accessories in original sealed packaging where applicable.

## 10. Product quality and reliability

Test samples from each batch: fit, charge speed, durability, heat. Track early failure reports in reviews. Defective electronics generate negative reviews, returns and potential safety concerns. Choose suppliers with consistent quality; avoid very low-cost items with unknown safety.

## 11. Economics in a low-price category

For a ₹300 accessory, shipping and packaging may be a large share. A 10% return rate hurts; so does discounting. Check contribution margin per variant (KB-06). Bundles (case + guard) raise order value if both are in stock.

## 12. Questions a KAM should ask

- What are the top return reasons for your accessories?
- Do your titles state the exact phone models or connector types?
- How do you check that the right model variant is packed?
- Can you prove the authenticity of branded items?
- What warranty do you give, and who handles claims?
- Does your category require IMEI or serial capture? Do you record it?
- Which models make up most of your sales?

## 13. What not to conclude

- Do not say an item is counterfeit or authentic without evidence.
- Do not state legal or platform requirements as facts.
- Do not claim a charging speed or compatibility that is not tested.
- Do not recommend stocking many model variants without model-level demand.
