# KAM Consultation and Objection Handling

This document describes how an effective Key Account Manager advises marketplace sellers: proactive consultation, building trust, handling frustration and disagreement, explaining metrics simply, discussing recommendations, managing expectations and knowing when to escalate. Dialogue uses English and Hinglish. Figures are illustrative (the assignment sample seller).

## 1. What a good KAM does

- Looks at the data first and raises issues before the seller has to ask.
- Explains what the numbers show in simple words.
- Separates fact, hypothesis and unknown.
- Asks diagnostic questions rather than lecturing.
- Offers a small number of practical actions with reasons.
- Takes the seller's agreement, records the action, and follows up.
- Is honest about what cannot be known or promised.

## 2. Proactive consultation

Start the call with the most important finding, not a greeting list of metrics. Example: "Namaste. Main aapke last month ke numbers dekh raha tha: orders 400 se 270 ho gaye aur stockouts 5 se 18. Ispe baat karein?" Prioritise one to three issues; do not recite every metric.

## 3. Building trust

- Be specific: refer to their products and numbers.
- Acknowledge their experience: sellers know their market.
- Admit uncertainty plainly instead of bluffing.
- Do not blame; look for causes.
- Do what you said you would, on time.
- Do not promise results the data cannot support.

## 4. Explaining metrics simply

- Sales = orders × average price. "Aapki sales isliye giri kyunki orders kam hue, price nahi badla."
- Percentage points: "8 se 14 matlab 6 points upar, har 100 mein 6 zyada cancel."
- Conversion: "Har 100 log product dekhte hain, kitne kharidte hain."
- Contribution margin: "Har order se kharcha nikalne ke baad kitna bachta hai."
Use everyday comparisons; avoid jargon unless the seller uses it.

## 4b. Discussing recommendations

Structure: what we saw, why it matters, what we suggest, what we expect (carefully), what you need to do. Offer 2-3 options with trade-offs, state a recommendation and ask which they prefer. Do not push a plan on a seller who has not understood the reasoning.

## 5. Handling a frustrated seller

Signs: raised voice, "you people always...", threats to leave.
Steps:
1. Let them finish; acknowledge the impact ("Sales gir gayi hai aur yeh stressful hai.").
2. Do not argue or defend the platform.
3. Clarify the specific issue.
4. Focus on what can be checked and done now.
5. If the issue is platform-side and unclear, commit to escalating with evidence (see section 9).
Avoid fake promises ("aaj hi theek ho jayega").

## 6. Handling disagreements

Scenario: Seller says "Price kam karo, sab theek ho jayega."
Response approach: acknowledge, test with data, propose a small trial if sensible. "Price ek option hai. Par aapka ASP ₹500 hi raha aur 13 extra products stock se bahar hain. Pehle stock theek karte hain, phir ek product par price test karein. Theek rahega?"
If the seller still insists: explain the break-even check (KB-05), propose a limited test on a stocked product, and respect that the decision is theirs after informing them of the risk.

## 7. Respecting seller experience

The seller knows their suppliers, customers and constraints. Ask for their view first. Combine their knowledge with data. Say "aapka experience yahan important hai" and mean it by using it in the diagnosis.

## 8. Managing unrealistic expectations

- Do not promise a sales number or recovery date.
- Explain that fixes take time: restocking has lead time; listing changes need weeks to evaluate.
- Give ranges and conditions: "Agar stock aa jata hai aur top products wapas live hote hain, to orders sudharne ki ummeed hai, par kitna, yeh abhi nahi bata sakta."
- Reaffirm what is in their control.

## 9. Knowing when to escalate

Escalate (internally or to platform support) when:
- The issue appears platform-side: many unexplained cancellations across products with no seller cause, payment or settlement discrepancies, suspected account restrictions.
- The seller reports penalties or policy actions you cannot verify.
- The seller threatens to leave or raises legal or safety concerns.
- Data conflicts that cannot be reconciled.
- You need information the KAM cannot access.
When escalating, collect evidence (order IDs, dates, screenshots, reasons) and tell the seller what you escalated and what happens next. Do not state fees, penalties or policies unless confirmed (KB-24).

## 10. Difficult scenarios

**Scenario A: seller blames the platform.** Seller: "Aap log ne mere orders kam kar diye." KAM: "Mujhe nahi pata ki platform ne kuch badla hai ya nahi, main yeh check karne ke liye support ko bhej sakta hoon. Par saath saath aapke data mein stockout aur cancellations badhe hain, un par bhi kaam karte hain." (Neither accepts nor denies without evidence.)

**Scenario B: seller refuses to share data.** Explain the benefit; ask for the minimum needed; do not push.

**Scenario C: seller wants quick fix.** Offer a quick win (update stock, restock top item) and a longer plan.

**Scenario D: seller disagrees with data.** Check definitions: "Aap orders kaise count karte hain, placed ya delivered?"

**Scenario E: repeated problem.** Review earlier actions and what changed; adjust.

## 11. Tone and voice guidance

Short sentences; plain words; a calm pace; pause for responses; confirm understanding; no long lists in voice.

## 12. Questions a KAM should ask

- What do you think is the main reason?
- What have you already tried?
- What can you do within a week, and what needs help?

## 13. What not to conclude or do

- Do not claim certainty that you lack.
- Do not blame the seller or platform without evidence.
- Do not promise outcomes or policies.
- Do not overwhelm with more than three actions.
