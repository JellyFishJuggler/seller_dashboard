# Search Visibility and Product Discoverability

This document explains how products get discovered on a marketplace and how to diagnose visibility problems. It is general guidance. **No ranking algorithm of Snapdeal (or any specific marketplace) is described or assumed.** What a platform actually uses to rank listings must be confirmed from its official documentation or support (KB-24); this document only describes factors generally known to matter in e-commerce search.

## 1. How products get discovered

Buyers find products through:
- Search queries typed into the marketplace.
- Category and filter browsing.
- Recommendations, "similar products", homepage and sale-event placements.
- Advertising placements (if the seller buys them).
- External links and brand searches.

A product is seen only if it is eligible to appear (live, in stock, correctly categorised) and relevant enough to rank for the query.

## 2. The visibility funnel

Impressions → clicks → product page views → orders.

- **Impressions**: how often the listing appeared.
- **CTR** = clicks ÷ impressions.
- **Conversion** = orders ÷ clicks.

Three different problems have different fixes:

| Pattern | What it suggests | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Low impressions | Not shown enough | Availability, category, title relevance, attributes, search demand |
| High impressions, low clicks | Shown but not attractive | Main image, title clarity, price, rating display |
| Good clicks, poor orders | Interested but not buying | Price, content mismatch, reviews, delivery, size/stock (KB-12) |

(This table is a compact summary; the three cases are explained below in prose.)

## 3. Low impressions

Possible causes:
- Product out of stock or inactive.
- Wrong or too-narrow category.
- Title lacking terms buyers search for.
- Missing attributes, so filters exclude the product.
- Low search demand for that product type (the product is simply not searched often).
- Heavy competition for the same terms.
- Policy or quality flags (platform-specific; confirm).
- A platform change affecting everyone (cannot be tested from the seller side).

Diagnostics: compare impressions across the seller's products and over time; check which products lost impressions; check stock status history; compare the title to how buyers describe the product.

Seller actions: fix availability, correct category and attributes, improve title with accurate buyer terms, complete content, and consider using advertising tools if the platform offers them (check cost against margin).

## 4. High impressions but low clicks

Possible causes: weak main image, unclear title, price displayed high versus alternatives, low rating shown, irrelevant impressions (the listing is appearing for loosely related searches).

Seller actions: improve main image, clarify title, check price against similar items, address rating issues. Test one change at a time.

## 5. Good clicks but poor orders

This is not a visibility problem; it is a conversion problem (KB-12). Interest exists, but the product page or terms stop the purchase.

## 6. Search relevance and product titles

Relevance is how well a listing matches a buyer's query. Buyers search by product type, use, size, material, brand, compatibility and sometimes Hinglish or local terms (for example "kurti", "pillow cover", "charging wire"). Titles and attributes should include accurate terms that buyers use. Misleading terms may produce clicks but returns.

## 7. Catalogue completeness and content

More complete listings are easier for any search system to understand and match. Fill all attributes, give clear descriptions and good images. See KB-10.

## 8. Search demand

If few buyers search for the product type, low impressions may reflect low demand, not a listing problem. Seasonal demand also changes search volume. Without search-volume data, avoid concluding.

## 9. Visibility diagnostics step by step

1. Confirm products are live and in stock during the period.
2. Compare impressions this period versus last by product.
3. Identify products with the biggest impression loss.
4. For those, compare CTR and conversion.
5. Check changes: title edits, price changes, stock gaps, category changes.
6. Separate seller-wide changes from product-specific ones.
7. If seller-wide and nothing changed on the seller side, consider market/season or a platform-wide change and ask seller support.

## 10. The sample seller

For the assignment sample seller, no impressions, clicks or conversion data was given. It is therefore not possible to say whether visibility changed. A KAM should ask for impressions and clicks if available rather than assume a ranking drop.

## 11. Improving visibility when impressions are low (action summary)

1. Make sure products are in stock and active.
2. Check category and attributes.
3. Rewrite titles with accurate, searchable terms.
4. Improve completeness and image quality.
5. Consider advertising for high-margin, in-stock products, with a budget cap.
6. Monitor impressions for 1-2 weeks after changes.

## 12. Questions a KAM should ask

- Which products have fewer views than before?
- Is the product in stock all days of the period?
- When did you last change the title, category or images?
- What do buyers search for to find this product?
- Do you run any paid placements?

## 13. What not to conclude

- Do not say "your ranking dropped" without data.
- Do not claim keywords X will raise rank.
- Do not describe a Snapdeal algorithm.
