# Conversion and Customer Experience

Conversion is the share of interested buyers who actually buy. This document explains the e-commerce funnel, why buyers hesitate, how to diagnose "interest without purchase", and what extra metrics are needed to confirm a conversion problem. Content is general; no platform data is assumed.

## 1. The purchase funnel

1. Impression (product is shown).
2. Click or product page view.
3. Consideration (reads title, images, price, reviews, delivery).
4. Add to cart or buy now.
5. Checkout and payment (or choose cash on delivery where offered).
6. Order placed.
7. Order accepted and delivered.

Conversion rate = orders ÷ product page visits (or clicks). Funnel leaks can occur at any step.

## 2. What a buyer evaluates on a product page

- **Price and perceived value**: compared with alternatives and with what the product seems worth.
- **Images and content**: do they show enough to decide?
- **Ratings and reviews**: social proof; recent review themes.
- **Delivery expectations**: promised delivery time and cost.
- **Trust signals**: clear brand, warranty, return information, authenticity.
- **Availability**: is my size, colour or model in stock?
- **Perceived risk**: fear of wrong size, damage, fake product.

## 3. Interest without purchase: diagnosis

The pattern: healthy clicks, few orders. Work through these hypotheses:

1. **Price too high for perceived value** (needs comparison with alternatives).
2. **Content weak or unclear** (small images, missing size/compatibility info).
3. **Poor ratings or recurring complaints** in reviews.
4. **Availability gaps**: the popular size/model is out of stock, buyers leave.
5. **Delivery promise or cost** unattractive.
6. **Expectation mismatch**: images suggest something the title/description contradicts.
7. **Wrong traffic**: clicks from irrelevant searches that never intended to buy.
8. **Checkout friction** (payment options, platform-side; cannot be changed by seller).

## 4. Metrics needed to confirm conversion problems

- Clicks or visits and orders per product (conversion rate over time).
- Impressions and CTR (to rule out irrelevant traffic).
- Product-level price compared with similar products (if available).
- Ratings, review count, review themes.
- Stock availability by variant over the same period.
- Delivery time promised and delivered.
- Cart additions versus orders, if available.

Without these, say that a conversion problem is a hypothesis.

## 5. Example (illustrative)

Product X: 20,000 impressions, 600 clicks (CTR 3%), 6 orders (conversion 1%). Last month: 600 clicks, 30 orders (5%). Clicks are unchanged; orders fell by 80%. Hypotheses: price change? stock in key sizes? new negative reviews? delivery promise longer? Ask what changed in the listing and what the recent reviews say.

## 6. Trust signals

- Honest descriptions and accurate images.
- Verified brand/authenticity where applicable.
- Warranty details if real.
- Consistent ratings and handling of complaints.
- Clear packaging and what's in the box.
Never fabricate or incentivise reviews (KB-16).

## 7. Reviews and ratings

Ratings influence trust strongly. A rating fall often follows a quality or packaging problem. Read review text for patterns. A few detailed negative reviews about the same issue are a signal even when the average rating looks fine. See KB-16.

## 8. Delivery expectations

Buyers want a clear and reasonable delivery estimate. Slow dispatch or inaccurate promises cause both lower conversion and more cancellations. Seller-side dispatch speed matters; delivery timing from couriers is partly outside seller control (KB-13).

## 9. Product expectation mismatch

A listing that overstates quality or leaves out size, colour or compatibility invites returns. Fix by aligning images, title and description with the real product. This may lower click-throughs slightly but raises kept orders.

## 10. Cart abandonment (where relevant)

On marketplaces, sellers usually do not see cart data. If available, cart-to-order drop-off suggests price, delivery cost or payment concerns. Do not assume.

## 11. Conversion improvement experiments

Test one change at a time and measure for a similar period:
- Main image replacement.
- Title clarity improvement.
- Adding a size chart or compatibility list.
- Adding in-use images.
- Adjusting price within margin limits.
- Ensuring stock for popular variants.
Measure conversion, returns and margin, not clicks alone.

## 12. Questions a KAM should ask

- Are people clicking but not buying?
- Have you changed price, images or content?
- What do reviews and returns say about this product?
- Is the popular size or model in stock?
- How do you compare to similar products in price and ratings?

## 13. What not to conclude

- Do not say conversion fell without clicks and order counts.
- Do not say price is the cause without comparison.
- Do not assume lower conversion is caused by seller error; part of the funnel is platform-controlled.
