# Business Action Planning and Prioritisation

This document teaches how to turn a diagnosis into a small set of agreed, trackable actions. It covers prioritisation, a standard action structure, recovery plans, ownership, deadlines, seller consent, follow-up and measuring outcomes. The sample seller (sales ₹2,00,000 to ₹1,35,000; orders 400 to 270; cancellations 8% to 14%; ASP ₹500; out-of-stock 5 to 18) is used as illustrative assignment data, not real Snapdeal data.

## 1. Why 2-3 actions

Sellers have limited time and cash. Many suggestions dilute effort and make results unattributable. Choose two or three actions that address the most likely and largest causes, have clear owners and can be measured. Park the rest as a "later" list.

## 2. Impact versus effort prioritisation

Score each candidate action:
- **Impact**: how much it could improve the key metric if the hypothesis is right (high, medium, low).
- **Confidence**: how strong the evidence is (KB-25).
- **Effort and cost**: time, money, difficulty.
- **Speed**: how soon results show.
- **Risk**: what could go wrong.

Pick actions with high impact and high confidence, low effort first ("quick wins"), then high-impact higher-effort items. Avoid high-effort, low-confidence actions until more evidence exists. If confidence is low, the right first action may be "gather data" (for example, pull cancellation reasons).

## 3. Immediate versus longer-term actions

- **Immediate (0-3 days)**: correct panel stock, pause promotions on unavailable items, review cancelled-order reasons, raise supplier orders for top products.
- **Short term (1-4 weeks)**: restock, fix listing content, adjust packaging, test a price change.
- **Long term (1-3 months)**: diversify assortment, supplier changes, process redesign, forecasting routine.

## 4. Standard action structure (use for every recommendation)

1. **Problem identified**
2. **Supporting evidence**
3. **Possible root cause** (label as hypothesis if not confirmed)
4. **Recommended action**
5. **Expected business impact** (directional, never invented numbers)
6. **Owner**
7. **Priority**
8. **Deadline**
9. **Success metric**
10. **Follow-up plan**

### Worked example (sample seller, illustrative)

**Action 1: Restock priority products**
1. Problem: Out-of-stock products rose from 5 to 18.
2. Evidence: Observed counts; sales and orders both fell 32.5% with ASP unchanged at ₹500, so the fall is in order volume.
3. Possible root cause (hypothesis): Several demand-heavy products are unavailable. Not yet confirmed; needs product-level order history.
4. Action: Rank the 18 out-of-stock products by past daily orders and margin (KB-09), and place restock orders for the top ones first.
5. Expected impact: If these products carried much of last month's orders, restocking should help recover orders; size unknown until history is checked.
6. Owner: Seller (restock); KAM (provide ranking).
7. Priority: High.
8. Deadline: Ranking within 2 days; restock orders placed within 3 days; target dates for stock arrival to be set from supplier lead times.
9. Success metric: Number of top-ranked products back in stock; daily orders on those products.
10. Follow-up: Check in after 3 days (orders placed?) and again after stock arrives (orders and cancellations recovered?).

**Action 2: Fix stock accuracy to reduce cancellations**
1. Problem: Cancellation rate rose from 8% to 14%.
2. Evidence: Observed rate; stockouts rose in the same period.
3. Possible root cause (hypothesis): Panel stock may not match physical stock, causing seller-side cancellations. Reasons data not yet reviewed.
4. Action: Review cancelled-order reasons; reconcile panel stock and physical stock for top products; update quantities; set a daily update routine.
5. Expected impact: Fewer seller-side cancellations if the hypothesis holds.
6. Owner: Seller.
7. Priority: High.
8. Deadline: Reconcile top products within 2 days; routine from then on.
9. Success metric: Cancellation rate and share of seller-side reasons.
10. Follow-up: Review reasons in one week.

**Action 3: Check visibility and conversion on top products**
1. Problem: Order volume fell; visibility and conversion are unknown.
2. Evidence: No impressions or clicks data yet.
3. Possible root cause: Unknown; to be tested.
4. Action: Gather impressions, clicks and order counts for top 5 products where available, or ask seller support for them.
5. Expected impact: Clarifies whether non-stock factors contributed.
6. Owner: Seller and KAM.
7. Priority: Medium.
8. Deadline: Within 5 days.
9. Success metric: Data obtained and reviewed.
10. Follow-up: Discuss findings in the next call.

## 5. Recovery plan types

- **Inventory recovery**: triage stockouts; restock by priority; sync stock (KB-09).
- **Pricing improvement**: test a price change on one product after break-even check (KB-05).
- **Cancellation reduction**: classify; fix stock sync; speed processing (KB-14).
- **Listing optimisation**: fix titles, images, attributes on top products (KB-10).
- **Returns reduction**: address top reason (KB-15).

Combine at most 2-3 per cycle.

## 6. Action ownership

Every action has an owner: the seller (decisions and operations), the KAM (analysis, follow-up, escalation to support), or a third party (supplier, courier, platform support). State the owner explicitly. If the action depends on platform support, the KAM or seller should raise it and note the expected reply time.

## 7. Deadlines

Make deadlines specific and realistic: tie them to lead times and workload. Use a date or "within N days" and convert to a calendar date when recording. Avoid open-ended "soon".

## 8. Seller consent

Do not log an action until the seller agrees. Ask: "Kya main yeh action aapke liye note kar loon? Owner aap, deadline Friday." If the seller declines or modifies it, record the modified version or leave it out. Consent confirms commitment and ownership.

## 9. Follow-up planning

Set the follow-up date when the action is created. Check: was it done, what changed, what is the metric now, do we continue, adjust or stop? Keep an action history so later calls can reference it.

## 10. Measuring outcomes

- Pick the success metric before the action.
- Compare before and after over equal periods.
- Allow time: restock and listing changes take days to weeks.
- Do not attribute all change to the action; note other changes (season, promotions).
- Record outcomes even when negative; learn.

## 11. Conversation script outline

1. Summarise the diagnosis in two sentences, with uncertainty.
2. Propose 2-3 actions, one by one, with a reason.
3. Ask for agreement on each.
4. Confirm owner, priority and deadline.
5. Say when you will follow up.

## 12. Questions a KAM should ask

- Which of these can you do this week?
- What is blocking you?
- What would make this plan unrealistic for you?

## 13. What not to conclude

- Do not promise impact numbers.
- Do not log actions the seller did not agree to.
- Do not make deadlines that ignore lead time.
